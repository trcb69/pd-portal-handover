The job file: paperless, or paper as now?

The production dashboard is designed and its foundation is built. How much it can show depends on one decision: whether the coordinator's job file moves into the dashboard, or stays a paper file carried from desk to desk. This paper compares the two, feature by feature.

For: CEO · COO · Production GM · managers and coordinatorsSources: SmartPack Zoho, September 2026 job sheet, the approved design (Rev 7.3)
The answer first
Paperless job file17 of 17dashboard features work, tracked from the order to the invoice

With the paper file, the dashboard only sees what Zoho sees: a sales order, then the packing, shipping and invoicing documents. Everything between the order and the shipment happens on paper, so the dashboard can't see it — and that is exactly where the time goes.

3 · Why it matters

What today's records already show

≈57%customised orders on time (Jul–Aug 2026)
67jobs past delivery date, not marked done (Sep sheet)
31%Screen Printing jobs blocked on raw material
<1%daily rows that say why a target was missed

One screen-printing pizza-box job was produced in 7 hours, inside a cycle of about four months from order to shipment. If that pattern holds, the time is lost before and after production: waiting for material, artwork and dies, and moving paper between desks. Those are exactly the steps a paper file hides.

4 · What changes for people

Who does what

Paperless

  • Coordinator: creates the file once in the dashboard, instead of paper + Excel + retyping; taps "send"; fills the reference sheet as things happen.
  • Manager: four actions only: receive, record output (preset quantity buttons), open or close a blocker, send back.
  • Ilham, Darshani: one reference number each, matched to Zoho automatically.
  • Analyst: stops rebuilding reports by hand.

Paper as now

  • Coordinator: same paper file, same Excel row, same Zoho order.
  • Manager: nothing new to enter, and nothing new to see beyond Zoho.
  • Everyone: "where is my order?" is still answered by a phone call.
  • Analyst: still rebuilds the reports from six registers each month.

What paperless needs

  • A device on the floor. Managers need a phone or tablet where the work happens. If they walk to an office PC for the 12:20 check, they will batch at day's end and the timestamps mean nothing.
  • The quality forms stay on paper at first. The inline inspection and QC visual checklists are controlled documents with numbers, revisions and signatures. They go digital only with a proper approval record, after the rest is stable.
  • A pilot before rollout. Screen Printing first: the highest pain and only 59 jobs. IT keys in the data for the first two weeks, so no one carries double entry while problems surface.
  • A transition with a printed cover. Until a division is fully paperless, the dashboard prints a cover sheet with a QR code for the file that still travels. The paper and the record stay linked, and nothing is lost in between.
5 · Recommendation

Go paperless, one division at a time

  1. Now: the foundation is built, reviewed and ready to deploy privately. It reads Zoho and gives the admin screens. This is the "paper as now" level.
  2. Next release: the order portal and the coordinator's digital job file, with send and receive taps. From here the file's location and the order's true start are known.
  3. Then: the manager workspace: blockers, output, incoming work, the 7 am list.
  4. Pilot: Screen Printing, two weeks, IT keying; then hand over to its manager; then one division at a time.
  5. Last: Stores and invoicing screens, the GM and COO reports, and the floor tablets.

Keeping the paper file keeps today's blind spot. The dashboard would show the Zoho paperwork faster, but not the four months between the order and the shipment, which is the part everyone is asking about.
